# Anchor Notes — Shape Up pitches

Study of Basecamp's Shape Up, Ch. 6 "Write the Pitch" plus the two real pitch examples
("To-Do Groups", Jason Fried; "Group Notifications", Ryan Singer). These notes feed
`prompt.py`; the blind comparison at close is against this register.

## Pitch skeleton (adjacent shape, not copied)

Problem (a single specific story showing why the status quo fails, giving a baseline) →
Appetite (start with a number, end with a design — not an estimate) → Solution →
Rabbit holes (named uncertainty) → No-gos (what we're deliberately not doing).

Groundwork mapping: What We Heard = Problem-as-evidence. Effort S/M/L per opportunity =
appetite, not estimate. Recommended Path = solution with the smallest first slice.
Open Questions = rabbit holes. Honest No's = no-gos.

## Honesty mechanics to enforce in the prompt

1. **Problem is shown, attributable, timestamped** — a quoted moment from the call, never "users struggle with X".
2. **The client's own workaround is stated respectfully**: "yeah it works, but it's messy" — admitting the hack works earns trust.
3. **Kill alternatives on the page**: each honest no walks through why the appealing option loses ("that increases the scope"; "adding functionality for completeness' sake is a bad product design smell").
4. **Use evidence against your own case** when it cuts that way ("we're the outliers here — it's not a problem").
5. **Open questions carry a best guess** ("I'd say yes, but we have to work that out") — not bare question marks.
6. **Concede when a cut thing might be better**: "X might be better in some people's eyes, but given the appetite it was important to mark this as a no-go."
7. **Plain personal vernacular**; conspicuously absent: superlatives, "seamless/robust/powerful", ROI theater, precision theater (no "37% improvement" without a stated basis), feature-count padding.
8. **Benefit framed as absence of harm** where true: "nothing changes if you don't use it."

## Anti-patterns (book's own warnings)

- Solution without a specific problem = no test of fitness.
- Problem without a solution = not ready to pitch (maps to: every opportunity needs its first concrete slice).
- Unconstrained scope: "anybody can suggest expensive and complicated solutions."
- Over-specified detail that boxes in the builder — leave latitude explicit.
